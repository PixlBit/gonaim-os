REFERENCE ASSIGNMENTS

@outdoor controls the exact corridor outside HIXXA's room: wall colour, framed pictures,
wooden floorboards, the runner rug, the ceiling beam, her door and the stairwell at the far end.
It does not control the camera angle.

@char_hixxa controls HIXXA's exact single full-body identity, skin tone,
body proportions, curly hairstyle and established wardrobe.
It does not control the camera angle, the shot size or her moment-to-moment expression.

@hixa-face reinforces only the face of that same single HIXXA.
It never creates a second person.

@dragon controls the exact dragon identity: serpentine neck, wing structure, limb proportions
and head shape. It never appears as a reference sheet or drawn page inside the world.

@weaponrazor controls the exact pair of long slender lances: shaft length, taper,
fine point and grip wrapping. Exactly two lances exist; they never become swords
and never multiply into three.

@razor controls the exact spined pale figure: plate shapes, rigid wing spans,
proportions and stance. It never flies above ceiling height and never leaves the corridor.

HANDS

Her hands are a working artist's hands: five fingers with one opposing thumb, correct joint
count and proportion, natural knuckle relief and tendon lines, fingers resting in a soft
natural curl, and grips that close with real contact against what they hold.

Dried paint is worn into the skin from long working days — soft pale blue and cyan along the
sides of the thumb and across the finger pads, a little dusty pink, cream flecks over the
knuckles. It is matte, faded and absorbed, following the skin's own creases and sitting under
the shading rather than on top of it. The fingernails stay clean bare nail: no colour on the
nail plate. The stains live on skin only.

SCALE

HIXXA is about 165 centimetres and her hand is the scale reference; where a stated size and a
hand-relative anchor seem to disagree, the anchor wins.

Each lance is longer than Razor is tall and finger-thin along its length.
Razor stands shorter than HIXXA, with a spined span half again its own height.
The dragon is long enough to coil around the ceiling beam but never fills the room.

CONTINUITY MAP

World geography: HIXXA's door is world-right at the near end of the corridor.
The stairwell and its cool daylight leak are at the far end.
The framed pictures stay on the world-left wall. One ceiling beam crosses the corridor
two thirds of the way toward the stairs. Never mirror, rotate or rebuild this geography.

HIXXA begins standing in her own doorway, her anatomical left hand on the frame,
her weight even, one small red pencil in her curls. She does not leave the doorway.

The dragon progresses through one continuous state:
[coiled along the ceiling beam, tail hanging] → [head lowered and tracking]
→ [dropped to corridor floor level] → [passing Razor] → [turning in the air]
→ [landed on the stair rail, wings folding].

Razor progresses through one continuous state:
[standing under the beam, both lances held low] → [points scoring the floor]
→ [pivoted with wings flared] → [one lance turned flat against the dragon's flank]
→ [carried through one shared turn] → [planted with both lance points aimed at the floor].

Both lance points are aimed downward at the film's end and stay that way.
Each shot begins at the exact next physical instant inherited from the previous shot.
No repeated action, pose reset, teleportation, wardrobe reset or unexplained time jump.

TIMELINE
################################################################

SHOT 01 — 0.00–2.20
24mm LOW CORRIDOR WIDE, camera near the floor at the near end.

Frame zero already shows the dragon coiled along the ceiling beam two thirds down the corridor,
its tail hanging free and swinging in a slow narrow arc. Warm fixture light reaches its
underside; the cool stairwell leak outlines its back. Dust drifts through both.

Hold the camera fixed.
End with the tail at the far side of its arc and the dragon otherwise still.

HARD CUT ON THE TAIL REACHING ITS LIMIT.

SHOT 02 — 2.20–4.00
70mm CLOSE on the dragon's head against the beam.

Inherit the swinging tail. The head lowers on the long neck until it hangs below the beam,
and the eyes come down to corridor level and fix on something beneath.
The neck plates slide over each other as it descends.

Extremely subtle forward drift.
End with the head fully lowered and the gaze locked downward.

HARD CUT ON THE GAZE LANDING.

SHOT 03 — 4.00–6.20
40mm MEDIUM WIDE at floor level, Razor centre frame under the beam.

Inherit the dragon's downward gaze from above and out of frame. Razor stands square on the
boards with both lances held low and angled back. Its rigid wings are folded tight.
It lifts its head to meet the gaze without moving its feet.

Hold fixed.
End with its head fully raised and both lances still low.

HARD CUT ON THE HEAD RAISING.

SHOT 04 — 6.20–8.40
90mm MACRO INSERT on the lance points and the floorboards.

Inherit the low lances. Both fine points drag backward across the wood and cut two shallow
bright score lines into the boards, raising thin curls of pale shaving.
The grip wrapping tightens visibly as its hands close.

Hold fixed.
The macro perspective preserves real surface relief and tactile separation between the tempered
points, the cut board grain and the curled shavings standing off the floor.

End with the two score lines complete and the shavings still curled on the boards.

HARD CUT ON THE SCORE LINES ENDING.

SHOT 05 — 8.40–11.00
35mm CORRIDOR WIDE from the near end, beam in the upper third.

Inherit the scored floor. The dragon releases the beam and drops, opening its wings once
as it falls. The displaced air runs the length of the corridor: the runner rug lifts along
its centre line, the framed pictures knock against the world-left wall and dust rolls forward.

Small retreat as the air arrives.
End with the dragon level with the corridor at chest height and its wings at full spread.

HARD CUT ON THE WINGS REACHING FULL SPREAD.

SHOT 06 — 11.00–13.40
28mm LOW WIDE behind Razor's heels.

Inherit the dragon's approach. Razor pivots on its planted right foot, its rigid wings flare
to brace against the incoming air, and its left lance comes up across its body.
Its heel digs a short groove in the board seam.

Hold fixed.
End with the pivot complete, wings flared and the left lance raised across the chest.

HARD CUT ON THE PIVOT CLOSING.

SHOT 07 — 13.40–15.80
50mm SIDE MEDIUM, camera against the world-left wall.

Inherit the raised lance and the dragon arriving in profile. At the last instant Razor rolls
its wrist and turns the lance flat, presenting the shaft rather than the point.
The flank meets the flat of the shaft and slides along it. The lance bows under the load
and does not break. No cut is made.

Hold fixed.
End with the flank halfway along the bowed shaft and the point angled clear of the body.

HARD CUT ON THE FLAT CONTACT.

SHOT 08 — 15.80–18.40
70mm CLOSE on the shaft, the wing and both grips.

Inherit the sliding contact. The dragon's leading wing hooks over the shaft.
Both bodies decelerate together: Razor's feet skid and the dragon's wingbeat shortens.
The bowed shaft straightens as the load comes off.

Slow track with the deceleration.
End with the wing still hooked and both of them nearly stopped.

HARD CUT ON THE STRAIGHTENING SHAFT.

SHOT 09 — 18.40–21.20
24mm DEEP CORRIDOR, camera at the near end, stairwell beyond.

Inherit the hooked wing. Carried by what is left of the momentum, the two turn once around
each other down the length of the corridor, Razor's feet leaving the boards for less than
a second before finding them again. The rug drags with them and the pictures swing.

Slow track forward, one continuous move.
The dimensional tracking move reveals restrained parallax between the two turning bodies, the
wall pictures and the stairwell beyond, and both hold constant volume through the whole turn.

End with the turn completed and both of them facing the stairwell.

HARD CUT ON THEIR FEET RETURNING TO THE BOARDS.

SHOT 10 — 21.20–24.00
40mm MEDIUM WIDE at the far end, stair rail in frame.

Inherit the completed turn. The dragon lifts, settles onto the stair rail and closes its
claws around it; the rail takes the weight and flexes once. Razor plants both feet,
lowers both lances and turns the points down toward the floorboards.

Hold fixed.
End with the dragon perched and steady and both lance points aimed at the floor.

HARD CUT ON THE POINTS TURNING DOWN.

SHOT 11 — 24.00–27.00
85mm PORTRAIT of HIXXA in her doorway, warm fixture light on her cheek.

Inherit the stillness at the far end. Her hand comes off the door frame. She releases the
breath she has been holding, her shoulders drop and her jaw softens.
Her eyes stay open and on them. No dialogue, no lip movement.

Extremely subtle forward drift.
Keep the face fully dimensional through stable cheek volume, nose projection, jaw structure,
eyelid depth and natural light wrapping.

End with her shoulders fully down and one curl still settling against her cheek.

HARD CUT ON HER SHOULDERS SETTLING.

FINAL SHOT — 27.00–30.00
32mm CORRIDOR MEDIUM WIDE from the near end, both figures in the cool stairwell leak.

Inherit the perched dragon and the lowered lances. The dragon folds its wings the last part
of the way and lets its neck curve down. Razor's stance eases a few centimetres without
lifting either lance. Dust settles through the cool light onto the dragged rug.

End on a locked stable frame: the dragon perched on the stair rail with wings folded,
Razor standing on the boards with both lance points down, two score lines and one heel groove
in the floor, the pictures hanging crooked on the world-left wall, and HIXXA in her lit doorway
with her hands at her sides and the red pencil in her curls.

################################################################
PHYSICS AND PERFORMANCE
################################################################

All movement is grounded: real foot contact, believable balance, force travelling from the
contact point through the body into the floor, natural recovery and restrained micro-motion.

The dragon has mass. It falls under gravity, its wingbeats displace real air and everything
loose in the corridor answers that air. The lances are stiff but not unbreakable: under load
the shaft bows visibly and straightens as the load comes off.
Razor's rigid wings brace and do not flap decoratively.
The stair rail flexes once under the landing and holds.
Wood scores, shavings curl and stay where they fall. Dust settles under gravity.

No object moves before direct contact or a visible physical force.
The lance points never touch the dragon at any moment of the film.

################################################################
MOOD AND RENDER CONTRACT
################################################################

Preserve the established HIXXA dimensional hand-painted cinematic animation language.

The image is fully constructed in three-dimensional space: volumetric characters,
modeled facial planes, rounded body forms, dimensional architecture, real perspective,
physical occlusion, natural parallax and clear foreground-to-background separation.

Render every character and object with convincing sculptural volume comparable to
high-end 3D animation, while treating every visible surface through controlled
hand-painted illustration: clean illustrated shapes, softly brushed shading,
painterly color transitions, subtly drawn edges, tactile material variation and
restrained authored texture.

Skin, curls, clothing fabric, floorboards, painted plaster, the lances' tempered metal,
Razor's pale shell plates and the dragon's layered hide and wing membrane remain materially
distinct and physically dimensional.

The result feels like a richly hand-painted cinematic frame occupying real
three-dimensional space: never flat 2D, never a paper cutout, never photoreal live
action and never glossy plastic or toy-like CGI.

Maintain stable facial volume, eye size, curl mass, body proportions, wardrobe
construction and material response across every lens and angle.

The dragon holds its volume in flight: the wing membrane catches transmitted light and shows
its internal structure, and the body's mass stays consistent through every change of angle.

################################################################
LIGHTING AND VISUAL CONTINUITY
################################################################

One warm ceiling fixture near HIXXA's door remains the dominant source at the near end;
a cooler daylight leak arrives from the stairwell at the far end.
The world direction, color temperature, exposure and time of day of both remain unchanged
across every angle. The warm key sculpts her cheek planes and curls at the near end while
raking light along the floorboards holds the corridor's depth readable from every angle.
As the action travels toward the stairs the pale plates move from warm to cool light;
this is a positional change, not a lighting change.

Use soft directional shadow, stable contact shadows and restrained atmospheric
perspective to preserve sculptural volume and real dimensional depth.

Colors remain rich and controlled without neon glow or excessive saturation.

No flat 2D or paper-cutout motion. No photoreal live action. No glossy plastic or
toy-like CGI. No waxy skin, generic game-render materials, watercolor bleed,
unmotivated neon lighting, heavy grain or style drift between shots.
No glow, no motion trails, no speed lines and no impact flashes.
