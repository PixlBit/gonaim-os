REFERENCE ASSIGNMENTS

@char_hixxa controls HIXXA's exact single full-body identity, skin tone, body proportions,

@hixa-face reinforces only the face of that same single HIXXA: large brown eyes, freckles
and facial structure. It never creates a second person.

HANDS AND PAINT STAINS

Both of HIXXA's hands carry permanent dried paint stains from long working days, and those
stains are part of her identity in every shot: soft pale-blue and cyan patches worn into the
sides of the thumb and across the finger pads, a smaller lilac or dusty-pink patch near one or
two nail beds, scattered cream and white flecks over the knuckles and the backs of the
fingers, and faint colour caught in the cuticle edges.

They read as dried, absorbed and worn — thin, matte, slightly faded, and following the skin's
own creases and folds. They are never wet paint, never fresh droplets, never glossy, raised,
wet-looking or glowing. The stains sit beneath the skin's hand-painted shading rather than on
top of it, so the hand keeps its full sculptural volume, knuckle relief and tendon structure.

The nails stay clean and natural with a visible pale nail plate; colour gathers around the
nail edges and cuticles, not across the nail surface.

The pattern is soft and irregular and never resolves into a graphic shape, a logo, a glove, a
tattoo, a bruise, a wound or dirt. Its placement, colours and density stay consistent across
every shot, lens and angle, and it never washes off, spreads or changes colour within a film.

PROP SCALE LOCK

HIXXA stands about 165 centimetres tall and her hand is the scale reference for this entire
film. Every object below is locked to both a real dimension and a hand-relative anchor.
Where the two seem to disagree, the hand-relative anchor wins. No object is rendered larger or
smaller than its stated relationship to her hand or body, and no object changes size between
shots, lenses or angles.

PENCIL CUP — a metal cup about 10 centimetres tall and 9 across, standing on the desk within
her hand's grasp. It is never a bucket and never a wastebasket.

GRAPHITE DRAWING PENCIL — a standard full-length pencil about 17 centimetres long and 7
millimetres across; held in her grip the tip clears her fingers by about a palm's width.

DESK TOOLS — brushes and pens standing in the cup are all between 14 and 20 centimetres long;
none is taller than the cup by more than half its own length.

SMALL RED PENCIL IN HER CURLS — a short stub about 9 centimetres long and pencil-thin,
roughly the length of her index finger. It never becomes a full-length pencil, brush or rod.

SKETCH SHEET — one landscape sheet about 30 centimetres wide and 21 tall, a little wider than
her two hands set side by side. It is never poster-sized and never notebook-small.

@desk controls the exact desk surface, wood grain, the metal pencil cup and its permanent
tools. It does not introduce any additional device, interface or new drawing surface.

CONTINUITY MAP

The world geography is fixed: the desk and its pencil cup stay world-right, the sketchbook
page lies flat and centred on the desk, the pinned wall sketches stay behind her.
Never mirror, rotate or rebuild this geography.

HIXXA begins standing at the desk, facing world-right, her anatomical right arm already
driving down toward the pencil cup, her anatomical left hand open near the page,
one small red pencil held in her curls that is never touched in this film.

The pencils progress through one continuous state:
[three pencils standing among the tools in the cup]
→ [gripped together in her right fist] → [rising clear of the rim as one bundle]
→ [two flicked onto the desk, one retained] → [the retained pencil locked in a drawing grip].

The page progresses through one continuous state:
[perfectly blank] → [still perfectly blank] → [still perfectly blank at the final frame].

Each shot begins at the exact next physical instant inherited from the previous shot.
No repeated action, pose reset, teleportation, wardrobe reset or unexplained time jump.

################################################################
TIMELINE
################################################################

SHOT 01 — 0.00–1.60
24mm PENCIL-CUP INTERIOR POV, lens low inside the cup looking up past the standing tools.

Frame zero already shows her hand entering the cup mouth, fingers spread and descending fast.
Her anatomical right hand plunges the rest of the way and closes around three pencils in one
committed grip. The neighbouring tools are struck aside and knock against the metal wall of
the cup exactly once. The cup itself rocks on its base and settles; the camera does not shake.

Hold the camera fixed inside the cup.
The extreme wide interior perspective preserves real surface relief and tactile separation
between painted pencil barrels, bare graphite, brush ferrules and the scuffed metal wall.

End with three pencils fully enclosed in her fist and the fist beginning to rise.

HARD CUT ON THE GRIP CLOSING.

SHOT 02 — 1.60–3.35
24mm CONTINUING UPWARD FOLLOW, the rim of the cup passing through the lower frame.

Inherit the closed fist and the three pencils already travelling upward at the same angle
and the same speed. The bundle continues its rise without pausing; the tips clear the rim
and the remaining tools drop back into place behind them. Her forearm leads, wrist trailing
slightly, and the loose curls at her temple lag one beat behind the pull.

Track upward with the pencils, one continuous move.

End with all three pencil tips clear of the rim and the fist still rising,
grip and count exactly as inherited.

HARD CUT ON THE PENCILS CLEARING THE RIM.

SHOT 03 — 3.35–5.45
45mm HAND-AND-FACE ANGLE, her fist in the near foreground and her face beyond it.

Inherit the rising fist with three pencils and the same upward momentum.
Her wrist snaps once and flicks the two spare pencils out onto the desk, where they land,
roll a short distance against the grain and stop. Her remaining fingers close the third
pencil into a hard drawing grip. Her eyes drop to the page and her jaw sets; her lips press
briefly together. Her hoops swing once with the wrist snap and settle.

Extremely subtle forward drift.
Keep the face fully dimensional through stable cheek volume, nose projection, jaw structure,
eyelid depth and natural light wrapping.

End with exactly two pencils at rest on the desk, one pencil locked in her grip
and her gaze already on the blank page.

HARD CUT ON THE GRIP LOCKING.

FINAL SHOT — 5.45–8.00
40mm OVER-PAGE ANGLE, looking down across the sketchbook toward her approaching fist.

Inherit the locked drawing grip and her fixed downward gaze. Her fist drives the pencil down
toward the still-blank page while her anatomical left palm slaps down flat on the near corner
of the paper and holds it. The page takes the impact, flexes once and settles under her palm.
Her shoulder and elbow drive the movement; the pencil tip closes the last distance fast.

Hold the camera fixed.
The over-page perspective reveals restrained parallax between the descending fist,
the page surface and the desk beyond it without flattening the image.

End one frame before the pencil tip contacts the page: the page perfectly blank,
her left palm planted on the corner, the two spare pencils still at rest on the desk
and the pencil tip a fraction above the paper.
Hold this final state for a maximum of four frames as an editorial stop;
every earlier frame remains fully active.

################################################################
PHYSICS AND PERFORMANCE
################################################################

All movement is grounded and human: real hand weight, believable wrist and elbow mechanics,
shoulder contribution on the final drive, joint compression and natural recovery.
The energy is high but the speed stays natural — fast because she is decisive,

Pencils are rigid wooden rods with real mass: they knock, they roll on the grain and they

Her curls, hoops, shirt cuff and apron each lag one beat behind her body and then settle;

No object moves before direct contact or a visible physical force.
